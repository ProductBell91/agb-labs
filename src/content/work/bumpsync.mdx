## Pregnancy apps are built for one person

Having a baby is a two-person job. The apps aren't. The partner hears everything second-hand, and the real decisions (the name, the pram, who does the night feed) end up in WhatsApp.

## Names got couples in

Picking a name is the one decision every couple enjoys arguing about. Each parent swipes on their own phone. Only the names you both like show up.

<Decision chose="Launch with names only. It's fun, and it needs both of you." gaveUp="A full pregnancy tracker on day one." />

## Couples stayed. Solo users didn't.

A month after sign-up, **37%** of connected couples were still using BumpSync. Solo users: **4%**. Fewer than a third ever invited their partner.

That changed the roadmap. More features wouldn't fix retention. Getting the second parent in would.

## So 2.0 was built around the partner

- **Invite during setup**, tracked as its own funnel. It used to sit in settings.
- **A weekly email and nudges** timed to the week of pregnancy.
- **Shared baby tracking after the birth**: feeds, sleep, nappies and growth. Your partner sees the 3am feed without asking.
- **Products inside the prep lists**, where the decision is made, not on a separate offers page.

<Decision chose="Ship 2.0 as one release, against a fixed baseline." gaveUp="Early wins. Finished features sat waiting for weeks." tilt={1.4} />

## What live users broke

- **7 of 32** invited partners were being dropped into new-user setup. Caught by a performance review, reproduced with a scripted QA run, fixed.
- An early "lift" turned out to be iOS users on the old build. Every analytics event now carries the app version.
- A parent said the bedtime suggestion ignored the last nap. Now it doesn't.
